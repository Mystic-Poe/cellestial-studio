PART 1 — THE 19 FINDINGS OF THE FIRST LOOK

Finding 1 (BLOCKER then) — the fall: one star crashed. RULING: PARTLY. What was asked for is there; a hard cut is new.
  Evidence: second-look/fall/*.jpg, fall.txt, fall-cut.txt (tools/fall.mjs, fall-cut.mjs; real time, DevTools
  screencast, every fx.impact call logged in the page).
  - Every star strikes with its own burst. fx.impact was called 57 times from Virgo, 38 (desktop) / 33 (phone) from
    Aries, 72 from Sagittarius: the figure's own stars at full size, the stars of the other layers as small ones.
    One roots.growFrom, from the first strike. Seen in desk-virgo-dense.jpg, phone-virgo-dense.jpg,
    desk-aries-dense.jpg, desk-sagittarius-dense.jpg, phone-aries-whole.jpg.
  - They accelerate: nearly still for 0.25 s, then longer and longer steps, with trails (long on the phone, short on
    the desktop). The first strike is 0.35 to 0.56 s after the press, the last 1.1 to 1.2 s.
  - The soil rises under them without standing still; the half-second of black the first look saw is gone (the
    ground is in the lower 40% of the frame from +1.1 s and keeps climbing).
  WHAT IS LEFT
  a. A HARD CUT at about +1.0 to +1.15 s. The web of root-light that the bursts threw into the ground, a third of
     the screen wide and the brightest thing on screen, vanishes in ONE frame. desk-virgo-cut.jpg: +1123 ms mean
     light 19.2, +1134 ms 11.6 (40% of the screen's light gone in 11 ms; 23.3 at +1103). Sagittarius 21.8 -> 15.6
     in one frame; phone Virgo 21.5 -> 16.8. Cause, read in src/experience/fx/layer.ts: IMPACTS_MAX = 18, "past
     this many at once the oldest, which is all but spent, makes way". It is not spent: the far stars' forty small
     impacts all land within 150 ms of each other at the centre and push every large burst out while it is at
     half its life. So the crash does not grow into the mycelium: it is switched off, and one trunk is left.
     Fix: do not shift a live burst out. Either merge the far stars' impacts into a few (one per 100 ms at the
     centre), or drop the newest small one when the list is full, or raise the cap and draw the small ones as a
     spot only (no rays, no sparks), so the large bursts live out their 760 ms and fade.
  b. A flat white lozenge lies on the ground beside the head of the trunk from about +1.0 s to +1.8 s (those same
     forty small impacts, all at the screen's centre, drawn on top of each other): desk-virgo-whole.jpg +1200 to
     +1800, desk-aries-dense.jpg +966 on. It reads as a white smear, not as stars landing. Fix: spread the far
     stars' landings across the width they fall over, and cap the summed light so it cannot clip to flat white.
  c. At Sagittarius on the desktop the 25 bursts land within 350 px of each other and add up to one solid white
     mass 250 px wide for 0.25 s (desk-sagittarius-dense.jpg +672 to +840). Same fix: cap the sum.
  d. Frame times on the desktop during the fall are not 60 a second, on this machine's own GPU and CPU, with
     nothing recording: average 19 to 20 ms, 2 to 4 frames over 34 ms, worst 67 to 83 ms; the sky's governor has
     stepped down a level by the end at Virgo and Sagittarius. With the CPU slowed 4x: average 32 ms (30 a second),
     11 to 13 frames over 50 ms, worst 100 to 117 ms. The phone profile holds 16.7 ms unslowed and averages 20 to
     21 ms slowed 4x (worst 50). The first look measured three frames over 34 ms, worst 50, with one burst.
     Fix: the bursts are drawn on a full-screen 2D canvas with blur glows; draw the small ones cheaply (see a).
